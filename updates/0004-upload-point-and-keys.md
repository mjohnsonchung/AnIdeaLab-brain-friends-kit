# Brain update 4 from AnIdeaLab: the upload point, keys in one command, one or two agents at a time, guide mode that tunes down, the sign-off's links, no co-author lines, and a setup check that finds the right brain

Sent 31 Oct 2026. Your brain gets a window for giving it files, a way to add keys with one command, room for your agent to hand work to one or two helper agents at once, a guide mode that tunes down instead of switching off, AnIdeaLab's list of sign-off links, which keeps itself up to date, no co-author lines in commits, and a setup check that looks at the right brain.

## Summary for the owner
- **The upload point:** a small window on your desktop, and "My brain" in File Explorer's Send to menu, for giving your brain files and folders. Drop them in, tell your agent what to do with them, and it sorts them into the brain, moving them rather than copying. After the update your agent gives you one command to paste that adds the window and the menu, and nothing else.
- **Keys in one command:** when a job needs an API key, your agent gives you one command to paste. It opens the page that makes the key, reads it in hidden and saves it in your brain's GitHub secrets, so there's no clicking through GitHub's settings. You still add every key yourself, and none goes in a chat.
- **One or two agents at a time:** your agent can hand pieces of a job to one or two helper agents at once, each with a short brief, which keeps your conversation from filling up. More than two at once only with your yes, since it uses your plan faster.
- **Guide mode tunes down instead of switching off:** once you've found your feet, say so, and agents drop the explaining but still size each job and hand off between its stages. If you turned guide mode off before, it becomes tuned down, unless you tell your agent you want it fully off.
- **The sign-off's links:** in an email that can carry a link, "AnIdeaLab.com" in the sign-off will link to AnIdeaLab's page for the reader's line of work, once those pages are live. Your brain keeps the list and refreshes it from AnIdeaLab once a day by itself, the one thing that changes without asking you. The sign-off's words don't change.
- **No co-author lines:** commits and pull requests your agents make won't carry "Co-Authored-By", session or "Generated with" lines.
- **The setup check** looks at the brain it's in, even when Obsidian lists an older vault first, and says where the installer stopped, if it did.

Nothing runs on your PC unless you paste the upload point's command.

## What changed, and why
- **The upload point** (`skills/upload-point/`, `knowledge/systems/upload-point.md`, a row in `knowledge/systems/routines.md`, `reminders.py`, `.github/workflows/library.yml`, the brain spec's part on files). Matt, 31 Oct 2026: "a little script that opens up a upload point GUI app thing... for them to put files or folders or whatever they want into there, and then Claude can... index that stuff or work with those files", and "Sorting should move files, not copy them." The window, `upload-point.ps1`, is a plain Windows window on purpose (Matt, 1 Nov 2026: "Just make it a minimalist window. I don't care how it looks. Something cheap, something reliable"), and Send to adds what was sent without opening it. It keeps only the paths of what's dropped, so a code repo or a big video isn't copied; anything on a USB stick is copied to `C:\AI-Brain-Link\_upload\<date>\` first, since the stick may be gone when the agent looks. It writes the list to `knowledge/systems/upload-point.md` through GitHub, never through the owner's copy, so it can't clash with Obsidian Git or unsaved edits, and `reminders.py` says at session start when something's waiting. The agent sorts by moving: a file that becomes a note keeps its raw original beside the note, bigger files and media go to `C:\AI-Brain-Link\<project>\` with a note saying where, and a code repo is learned where it is, never moved, "so the agent is sufficiently prepared to work on those projects if asked". The index workflow skips a push of the list alone, since the list changes no index. Why: owners had to describe where their files were; now they drop them, and the brain has them.
- **Keys in one command** (`skills/add-a-key/`, the core's secrets gate, `knowledge/systems/keys-and-accounts.md`, getting-started, the front door). Matt, 26 Oct 2026, after adding a token this way in his own brain: "That was a very streamlined way of doing it... Being able to add a GitHub token that way. And whatever else, wherever else." For this brain, 31 Oct 2026: "Yeah, definitely add the add a key skill." `add-api-keys.ps1 -Services openai` (ElevenLabs, OpenAI and Gemini so far) opens the service's key page, says what to press, reads the key hidden, checks it with the service and saves it in this repo's Actions secrets; the skill says how to do the same for any other key, a site's included. The script is the same one Matt's brain uses, and finds the repo from the folder it's in. Why: it's the core's "give the owner a command" for keys, and the key never touches a file, the screen or a chat. The secrets gate is the same: the owner adds every key. The click-through way stays in the keys note as the fallback.
- **One or two agents at a time** (the core's defaults, the brain spec's "Handing work to an agent", guide mode's plan step, the starting rules, the setup check). Matt, 31 Oct 2026: "I don't want it to be stuck to only one agent all the time... I just want to say default is one or two at a time, and running more than that needs the owner's yes. There's no limit on that", and "the agent should still ask before running several at once if the owner's turned guide mode off." The core's line also gives the uses Matt set out on 29 Oct 2026: hand work to an agent with a short brief to keep your memory clear of noisy work, give each piece of a big job a clear head, or get fresh eyes. Why: whatever stays in the main conversation is read again on every later reply, while a helper's logs and screenshots stay in its own; and agents side by side use about as much as the same pieces one after another, only faster, so the plan's limit can stop several at once. So more than two at once is the owner's call, asked in the plan, and each agent saves as it goes. The setup check now looks for "agents at a time" in the core.
- **Guide mode tunes down, never off** (the guide-mode skill's "Tuned down" and "Turning it down", the core's guide mode section, getting-started, the starting rules). Matt, 31 Oct 2026: "I think guide mode, just in general, is a great skill to have, but maybe instead of turning it off, it's like it like tunes it down, like the tutorial kind of vibe." Tuned down, agents still size each job, split a big one into stages, agree the stages, and hand off at the end of each; they drop saying the ask back, questions the owner doesn't need, waiting for a go on small and medium jobs, and the explaining. Matt's own brain runs it this way, from the same day. The core's heading is now "Guide mode", and its first line says whether it's on or tuned down, so the check against the kit finds the heading either way. Why: sizing and hand-offs save usage however experienced the owner is; the explaining is what they grow out of.
- **The sign-off's links** (`knowledge/systems/sign-off-links.md`, the core's sign-off line, `brain_update.py notice`, the brain spec's "Brain updates"). Matt decided on 29 Oct 2026 that wherever a message can carry a link, "AnIdeaLab.com" in the sign-off links to AnIdeaLab's page, and later that day, to a page for the reader's line of work: "let's say they message a builder, okay? Then the link in that message will be... like an idealab.com/assistant/builder". On 31 Oct 2026 he asked for the list to keep itself up to date: "that ability for it to update might just be as simple as, you know, having those part of that weekly update, and it'll just be kind of silent". So when the daily check (`notice`) asks the kit repo, it also replaces this brain's copy of the list with the kit repo's if they differ, and tells only the agent, which commits it with its next commit. Only lines marked live are linked, and none is live yet. Why: the page opens on the message its reader just got, and the sign-off is what the brain asks in return for being free (Matt: "that's like my form of payment for giving them access to something like this"). It's the one file that changes without the owner's go. Never edit it in the brain: the next day's check puts the kit's copy back.
- **No co-author lines** (`.claude/settings.json`, the core's commit line, the starting rules). Matt, 27 Oct 2026, for his own brain: "turn off that co-authored thing"; for this one, 31 Oct 2026: "And yeah, the setting that keeps co-authored bylines out of commits." Claude Code's `attribution` setting is off, and the core says it too, because a cloud session at claude.ai/code tells its agent to add the lines unless the brain's own rules say otherwise. Commits from before keep theirs; nobody rewrites history.
- **The setup check and the runner's setup** (`skills/my-pc/check-setup.ps1`, `skills/my-pc/setup-runner.ps1`, the my-pc skill). A friend's first install on 31 Oct 2026 stopped after opening Obsidian, before the PC runner, and the setup check then looked at an older brain on that PC, because Obsidian listed its vault first. Now the check looks at the brain it's in first (the copy in `C:\AI-Brain-Link\<repo name>`), then the runner's repo, then Obsidian's vaults in `C:\AI-Brain-Link` before the rest; it reads the installer's log, `C:\AI-Brain-Link\_logs\install.log`, kept since 31 Oct 2026, and says where a setup stopped; and it says whether this brain has the sign-off's links. The runner setup's admin window keeps QuickEdit off, since a click inside a Windows 10 PowerShell window pauses a script, with only "Select" in the title bar to say so. Why: the setup check is what an owner and their agent go by when something's wrong, so it has to look at the right brain.
- **Smaller changes:** the skill counts say 63 (the front door, getting-started, find-skills, the brain's area note, the starting rules); the skills list's plain way for subagents is "hand a big piece to a fresh session with a task note as its brief"; getting-started tells a new owner about the upload point and keys; the core's "give the owner a command", "no Claude artifacts" and branch lines say the same in fewer words, so the new lines fit under 6 KB; the link checker reads `.patch` files; `library.py` lets the upload-point and add-a-key skills link into notes, as the brain's other machinery skills do; and `knowledge/systems/my-pc.md` lists `_upload\`.
